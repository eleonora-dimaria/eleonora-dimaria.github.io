Club Essenzia

Music & Nightlife

  • brand identity
  • illustration
  • art direction
  • social media design
  • poster design

Role

Freelance brand designer, identity built from scratch for a new project.

Challenge

Club Essenzia wasn’t only launching a music project, it wanted to build a community around nightlife as a safe, welcoming and inclusive space. The brand needed to express that from day one, and stay instantly recognizable across every event, with no existing asset to build on.

Research & Thinking

Instead of starting from a logo, I explored visual references and futuristic typography to find a symbolic direction that could express the project’s personality beyond the music itself.
That exploration led to building the identity around a distinctive illustrated character rather than a mark alone, since a character can evolve and appear across formats in a way a static logo can’t.

What I designed

  • A custom illustrated mascot as the central element of the identity
  • The full logo suite
  • Color palette & typography supporting the logo
  • Event posters (including for the Anti Capodanno event)
  • Instagram content and carousels
  • Merchandise illustration and the official artwork for the club’s t-shirts

Before → After

Before, this was a new music project with no visual identity and no recognizable presence. After, Club Essenzia has a distinctive, consistent identity applied across events, social media and merchandise, one that reinforces the sense of community the founders wanted to build.

Sideful

DJ Branding

  • brand identity
  • illustration
  • art direction
  • social media design
  • poster design

Role

Freelance brand designer. Visual identity for an electronic music project.

Challenge

Sideful needed a sign strong enough to be recognized at a glance and flexible enough to work on very different backgrounds, from flat brand colors to photographs of nights out and DJ sets.

Research & Thinking

The starting point was Neue Einstellung, a heavy display typeface, with some letters customized. The S is the most worked one: its continuous curve evokes sound waves and movement, and its two terminals are cut at the same 53° angle, mirroring each other. The U and the L were also adjusted: the U has a cut at the top of its left stem, set at the same 42° angle as the curved corner of the L, so the two letters echo each other. The rest of the letters stay more rigid and balance the S. The whole wordmark is built on a construction grid, where the height of the capitals equals four times the thickness of the stem of the I.

What I designed

  • A custom logotype based on Neue Einstellung, with its construction grid
  • A symbol, the S inside a circle, in four color combinations
  • A five-color palette (cream, warm black, powder blue, midnight blue and brick red), with approved logo pairings and combinations to avoid
  • A type system that uses Neue Einstellung for headlines, to stay consistent with the logo, and Work Sans for body text
  • An image direction built on grainy, warm-lit photography, where the S can also work as a decorative shape for textures, color effects and overlays
  • Usage guidelines, including what not to do with the logo

Before → After

Before, Sideful was an electronic music project without a defined visual language. After, it has a coherent identity made of logotype, symbol, palette, typography and image direction, one that holds up across very different backgrounds and photographs.

The Unique Group

Luxury Jewellery Exhibitions & High Jewellery Gallery

  • art direction
  • brand design
  • editorial design
  • social media design
  • web design
  • event branding

Role

Creative Project Manager & Head of Editorial Content (freelance), since January 2024. I take briefs directly from the founders and lead the visual identity and editorial output for the whole group: The Unique Show, its international exhibition circuit in Europe, and The Unique Gallery, a permanent high jewellery destination in Dubai.

Challenge

The Unique Show has run six editions across Monte-Carlo, Dubai, Como and Lugano, each with 30-60+ brands and 8,000+ visitors from 38 countries. Every edition has a new theme and new exhibitors. The Gallery, instead, is open year-round and needs the same authority all the time, not just around an event.

The goal: one recognizable identity across changing events, a permanent space, and formats from printed catalogues to Instagram.

Approach

I built the group’s core visual language (palette, typography, tone of voice, imagery) so every edition and every piece of content starts from the same foundation.

For each edition, I developed a new concept. For Luxury Monte-Carlo 2025, the collection book was organised by the exhibitors’ regions of origin, each section inspired by local culture and history, from El Dorado to the origins of pearls, with bespoke illustrations for each.

For the Gallery, I studied how other high jewellery brands communicate, to find a tone that feels credible rather than generic luxury.

What I designed

  • The group’s core visual language: palette, typography, tone of voice and imagery
  • Art direction and editorial content for the official event magazines
  • The Luxury Monte-Carlo 2025 collection book: a storytelling concept organised by region, with bespoke illustrations later adapted into large-scale exhibition totems
  • Wall installation graphics for The Unique Gallery in Dubai
  • A redesign of The Unique Show website, plus a blog built for search
  • Creative direction for the group’s social media

Results

The 2025 collection book was particularly well received, with recurring requests for reprints. Across the collaboration, 15,000+ magazines have been distributed in luxury venues internationally. After the redesign of The Unique Show website, organic clicks from search grew by 282% and overall traffic by 14x.

The Unique Group Website

Design and Content Management

  • web design
  • editorial design
  • content management
  • SEO
  • copywriting

Role

Freelance designer and Head of Editorial Content. Responsible for the website’s redesign, usability and editorial content.

Challenge

The website promotes Luxury Monte-Carlo, one of The Unique Group’s flagship events, and had to present a large amount of information: exhibitors, program, venue and practical details. That information was scattered and presented in a confusing way, so visitors struggled to find what they needed. On top of that, the site was not optimized for mobile or tablet. The challenge was to turn it into a clean, orderly website that guides the visitor from one piece of content to the next, on any device.

Approach

The work started from the content itself: information was sorted, grouped and put in a clear order, so that each page has one purpose and each path through the site is short. The layout was then built to work on desktop, tablet and mobile. On top of this structure, the visual direction was built around the event. A dark magenta, the color associated with Luxury Monte-Carlo, became the main color of the site, while palm tree shadows and small, restrained stars add atmosphere without competing with the content. Alongside the design, a blog was opened and is managed end to end: article planning and writing, image selection and SEO.

What I designed

  • A redesigned website, with its information reorganized into a clear structure
  • A layout optimized for mobile and tablet, which the previous site lacked
  • A visual language based on dark magenta, palm shadows and sober star details
  • A blog, run on an ongoing basis: articles, images and SEO

Screenshots show the design as originally delivered.

Results

In the three months after launch, organic search clicks grew by 282% and overall traffic increased 14x. The website is now orderly and easy to navigate, recognizable at first glance and consistent with the event it promotes, supported by a blog that keeps it active.
